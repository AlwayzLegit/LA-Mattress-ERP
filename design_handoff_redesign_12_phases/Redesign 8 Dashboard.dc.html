Phase 8 — Dashboard and owner reporting

Critique first, then all four role homes as one live prototype (Proto Dashboard.dc.html) — every existing card and function kept, cash pickups rebuilt.
8a
Critique of the shipped owner home

What answers the owner's first question: the Stores section (one card per store, written and collected, salesperson activity, money by tender, cash pickups) is the best thing on the page and stays. Written business gives the trend. Morning brief is the 10pm close in prose.

What does not: the six-tile KPI strip at the top makes six numbers equally loud, so none is first. Period and store scope live in the header and again on cards. The Changes card is the right content in the wrong place — it is the owner's audit tool, not a 9am read. The Staff schedule is useful but is a management task, not a morning number. Comparison baselines exist on some tiles and not others.

Jobs, on the shipped page: "Which number do I read first?" Nobody could answer in three seconds. That is the whole brief.

Decisions
  • One headline: company written today, with same day last week and same day last month beside it. Nothing else in that row.
  • Everything the owner already has stays, restyled to the Phase 2 system, in this order: headline → by store → by associate → receivables & refunds/cancellations by associate → 10pm close exceptions → written trend → changes → schedule.
  • Every tile carries a baseline. No number without one.
  • Onboarding collapses to one line once required steps are done, then disappears.
  • Manager variant is the same page scoped to one store, with the exception list first (per Phase 1).
Design notes

Ive wanted the headline alone on a white page with everything else below a fold. Forstall: the owner reads this on a laptop at a kitchen table; by-store must be visible without scrolling or the number has no story. Settled: headline row 120px tall, by-store immediately under it, both above 900px.

Chaudhri turned the six KPI tiles into a single row of small baseline-bearing figures under the headline — the same six numbers, one-fifth the height.

Later: the team's better idea — remove the period picker entirely and make the dashboard always "today, so far" with history in Reports — conflicts with the shipped month-to-date default and is recorded, not applied.

store manager · first question: Did the drawer balance, and what did the 10pm close flag?

The close runs automatically at 10pm by decision; this screen is the morning-after read. Six tiles (sales, gross, tax, refunds, net, order money) each carry the previous same-weekday figure. The cash-drawer table puts Over / short in the last column in bold, and an exception component sits directly under the offending row with two actions — recount or record with reason — rather than a status word.

Forstall wanted sign-off to be blocked while an exception is open. Jobs: the manager may not be able to resolve it (Sam has gone home); signing records that they saw it, and the exception stays open on the owner's list. Both truths on screen.
